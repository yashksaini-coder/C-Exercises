# 110 — Reverse words in place

Implement `void reverse_words(char *s)` in `110.c`.

Reverse the order of the words in `s`, in place, without allocating.
Words are runs of non-space characters separated by exactly one space;
assume no leading or trailing spaces and no consecutive spaces (the
contract is deliberately narrow so the trick stays visible). The
characters inside each word keep their order. A single word, or the
empty string, is unchanged.

The O(1)-space trick: reverse the entire string character by character
(`"hello world foo"` → `"oof dlrow olleh"`), then walk through it and
reverse each word in place (`"foo world hello"`). Write a `static` helper
`reverse_range(char *first, char *last)` and call it twice; you may reuse
your `reverse_string` from 026 by copying it in (it is not linked here).

No second buffer, no `malloc`, no `strtok`. `strlen` is allowed.

Examples: `"hello world foo"` → `"foo world hello"`; `"a b"` → `"b a"`;
`"single"` → `"single"`; `""` → `""`; `"ab cd ef gh"` → `"gh ef cd ab"`.

> [!TIP]
> Read: Beej's Guide to C, §7 Strings and §5 Pointers —
> https://beej.us/guide/bgc/html/split/strings.html
> The "reverse twice" idea is a staple; the interviewer is checking you
> can manipulate a string through pointers without an extra buffer.
