# 105 — Condition variables

Implement `BoundedQueue *bounded_queue_new(size_t capacity)`,
`void bounded_queue_push(BoundedQueue *queue, int value)`,
`int bounded_queue_pop(BoundedQueue *queue)` and
`void bounded_queue_free(BoundedQueue *queue)` in `105.c`.

A bounded queue is the producer/consumer primitive: producers `push`,
consumers `pop`, and when the queue is full a producer *waits* (rather than
failing or spinning) until a consumer makes room; when it is empty a
consumer waits until a producer adds something.

`BoundedQueue` is opaque. Inside: an `int` ring buffer of `capacity`
slots (head index, count), one `pthread_mutex_t`, and two
`pthread_cond_t`: `not_full` (producers wait on it) and `not_empty`
(consumers wait on it).

Contract:

- `bounded_queue_new(capacity)` allocates everything, initialises the
  mutex and both condition variables; `capacity == 0` or allocation
  failure → `NULL`.
- `bounded_queue_push(q, v)`: lock; `while (count == capacity)
  pthread_cond_wait(&not_full, &mutex)`; store at `(head + count) %
  capacity`; `count++`; `pthread_cond_signal(&not_empty)`; unlock.
- `bounded_queue_pop(q)`: lock; `while (count == 0) pthread_cond_wait(&not_empty,
  &mutex)`; take `items[head]`; advance `head`; `count--`;
  `pthread_cond_signal(&not_full)`; unlock; return the value.
- `bounded_queue_free(q)`: destroy both condition variables and the mutex,
  free the buffer and the struct; `NULL` is a no-op. The caller promises no
  thread is blocked in the queue at that point.

The two details that separate a correct implementation from one that
"usually works": the wait must be in a `while`, not an `if`, because
`pthread_cond_wait` can wake spuriously and because another thread may
grab the slot between the signal and your reacquiring the lock; and you
signal the *other* side's condition variable after every change. FIFO
order must be preserved among items from one producer.

No global state; no sleeping or busy-waiting (`sched_yield`/`usleep` loops
are forbidden). The test uses capacity 4 with 2 producers and 2 consumers
moving 2000 items, so blocking really happens on both sides.

Examples: capacity 2, push 1, push 2, pop → `1`, pop → `2`; one thread
pushes 0..9 into a capacity-4 queue while the main thread pops ten values →
`0, 1, ..., 9` in order; 2 producers × 1000 items (values 0..999 each) and
2 consumers → consumers' totals sum to `2 × 499500`;
`bounded_queue_new(0)` → `NULL`.
