# 004 — Greatest common divisor

Implement `int gcd(int a, int b)` in `004.c`.

Return the greatest common divisor of `a` and `b` using Euclid's algorithm:
while `b` is not zero, replace `(a, b)` with `(b, a % b)`; the answer is the
final `a`. The result is always non-negative. Negative inputs are allowed
and are treated by magnitude: `gcd(-12, 18)` is `6`. By convention
`gcd(0, 0)` is `0` and `gcd(0, n)` is `|n|`.

Work in `int` and take absolute values yourself (do not call `abs`, and no
`<math.h>`). `INT_MIN` is out of scope: its magnitude does not fit in an
`int`, and the tests never pass it. The pitfall to notice: `%` in C keeps the
sign of the left operand, so if you forget to normalise the inputs, a loop
on negatives can finish with a negative answer.

Examples: `gcd(48, 18)` → `6`; `gcd(17, 5)` → `1`; `gcd(0, 9)` → `9`;
`gcd(-12, 18)` → `6`; `gcd(0, 0)` → `0`.

> Read: Beej's Guide to C, §3.5 The `while` statement —
> https://beej.us/guide/bgc/html/split/variables-and-statements.html
> Euclid's algorithm is the standard example of a loop with a shrinking
> invariant.
